Caring for skin as the seasons turn cold

Cooler air, central heating and long days can leave skin feeling tight. A few simple swaps keep your routine comfortable all winter.

When temperatures drop, many of us notice our skin behaving differently. Cold air outdoors and dry, heated air indoors can make the complexion feel tighter and look a little duller than in summer. The good news: you rarely need a whole new routine — just a few thoughtful adjustments.

Cleanse more gently

If your cleanser leaves skin feeling squeaky, that's a sign to soften your approach. A cushiony foam like our Gentle Foaming Cleanser with lukewarm (never hot) water helps skin feel clean and comfortable.

Layer hydration

Rather than reaching straight for the richest cream, try layering: a water-light serum first, then a moisturizer to seal it in. Thin layers absorb better and feel lighter than one heavy one.

Swap to a richer night cream

Evenings are the perfect time for a more nourishing texture. A velvety formula such as Midnight Renewal Night Cream leaves skin feeling soft and comforted by morning.

Don't forget hands

Hands are often the first to feel the cold. Keep a travel-size hand cream in your bag and apply after every wash.

Good to know

Sunscreen still matters in winter, especially on bright days. And if your skin feels persistently uncomfortable, a professional is the best person to advise.

The five-minute morning ritual

Four steps, in the right order, for skin that feels fresh and ready for the day.

A good morning routine doesn't need to be long. It needs to be in the right order: thinnest textures first, richest last — so each layer can do its job.

  1. Cleanse. A gentle foam wakes skin up without stripping it. Rinse with cool water for an extra refreshing start.
  2. Hydrate. Press two or three drops of a water-light serum such as Rose Hydration Serum into damp skin.
  3. Refresh the eye area. A cooling applicator, like that of our Revitalizing Eye Serum, is a lovely way to help the eyes look more awake.
  4. Moisturize. Finish with a fresh gel cream like Hydra-Veil — then sunscreen as your final step.

Give each layer thirty seconds to settle before the next. That small pause is what turns a routine into a ritual.

An evening ritual for rested-looking skin

Why the evening is the best time to slow down — and how to make it count.

In the morning we're often rushing. The evening is different: there's time to take a breath, and skin has the whole night to enjoy richer textures without makeup or sunscreen on top.

Take your time to cleanse

Massage your cleanser in for a full minute. It's the simplest way to make sure the day's makeup and sunscreen are lifted away — and it feels wonderful.

Treat

A treatment serum is the heart of an evening routine. Our Dual Botanical Serum blends a water phase and an oil phase fresh with every pump, for skin that looks smoother and more radiant.

Nourish

Seal everything in with a comforting night cream. Warm it between your palms first and press — rather than rub — it into the skin.

Once a week: a mask moment

Set aside ten minutes for a mask, a cup of tea and absolutely nothing else. Your skin will feel soft afterwards — and so will you.

Hydration, explained

Humectants, emollients and oils: what each one does, and why most skin enjoys all three.

“Hydrating” is one of the most-used words in skincare — but it covers a few different jobs. Understanding them makes it much easier to choose the right textures.

Humectants
Ingredients such as hyaluronic acid and glycerin help draw water to the skin's surface. They're the backbone of light serums and gel creams, and help skin feel plump and supple.
Emollients
Squalane, shea butter and similar ingredients fill in the tiny gaps on the skin's surface, so it feels smooth and soft to the touch.
Oils
Plant oils like jojoba, sweet almond and evening primrose add a nourishing, cushioned feel and help seal in the layers beneath.

Most comfortable routines use all three: a humectant-rich serum, a moisturizer with emollients, and — when skin wants extra comfort — a few drops of oil or a richer cream at night.
